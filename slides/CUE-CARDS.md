## Slide 5 — Every cleaning decision is logged, because each one changes the answer
**4:00–4:55 (55 s)** · **Speaker:** Patten Lai

**On screen**
- Returns and cancellations are behaviour, not noise: removed from spend, kept as a return-rate feature.
- Service codes (postage, bank charges, discounts) are excluded from product-level analysis only.
- The audit counts exceed the funnel removals because one line can violate several rules — both are reported.

**Say (verbatim from the plan):**

> Three principles guided cleaning. First, never silently drop information: every removal is counted and reported, and the anonymous revenue share is quantified. Second, separate description from prediction: we remove rows that cannot be attributed to a customer from the modelling population, but keep them in the audit so the business picture is not understated. Third, returns are real behaviour — a customer who returns goods behaves differently — so they leave the spend calculation but stay as a feature. The table shows the audit counts; the funnel removes slightly fewer because one line can violate several rules at once, and we report both numbers.

## Slide 8 — From 16 behavioural signals we keep 10 — a small but measurable gain
**6:35–7:40 (65 s)** · **Speaker:** Weng Zhi Chao

**On screen**
- RFM alone is not enough: regularity, returns and revenue momentum separate a loyal low-spend customer from a lapsing high-spend one.
- Selection is a documented funnel: correlation filter (|r| > 0.9) → mutual information top-12 → RFE top-10.
- We keep the smaller set because it is cheaper and slightly better on validation; the gain is honest, not dramatic.

**Say (verbatim from the plan):**

> RFM features are the starting point, not the answer. We add regularity, returns, momentum and basket structure so that a loyal low-spend customer is distinguishable from a high-spend customer who is lapsing. Spend is heavy-tailed, so everything is log-transformed and scaled before any distance-based method. Selection is a three-stage funnel: correlation removes near-duplicates, mutual information catches non-linear signal, and recursive elimination keeps the final ten. The honest result: validation PR-AUC moves from 0.6966 to 0.7036. Small — but measured, and the smaller model is cheaper to run.

**Say (verbatim from the plan):**

A segmentation that changes every time the data refreshes is not usable by a marketing team, so stability is the criterion we optimise.

## Slide 13 — Where the model fails — and why we accept it
**12:35–13:45 (70 s)** · **Speaker:** Lim Man Tung Marcus

**On screen**
- False negatives are expensive in lost revenue; false positives are expensive in contact cost — the threshold is a budget decision.
- The label is a proxy: a customer with no purchase in 90 days may be on holiday, not lost.
- One out-of-time period cannot tell us how much AUC would vary across future quarters; we say this rather than average it away.

**Visual:** results/figures/f09_confusion_and_threshold.png

**Say (verbatim from the plan):**

> No model is right all the time, so we show the failure modes. At the F1 threshold the confusion matrix trades false negatives — customers we should have contacted but missed — against false positives, which are wasted contacts. That trade-off is a budget decision, not a statistical one, which is why we report both thresholds. We also state two honest limits: the label is a ninety-day no-purchase proxy, and a single out-of-time period cannot estimate quarter-to-quarter variance. Being explicit about error is part of the deliverable, not a weakness.
